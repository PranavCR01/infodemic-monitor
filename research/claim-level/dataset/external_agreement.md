# Source labels vs. the derived video label (batch 2)

Generated by `scripts/compare_external.py` from `videos.csv` (`external_label`, the source's own label verbatim; `video_level_label`, derived by the section 9 rules) and `sources/batch2_selection.csv` (`external_tags`). Do not edit by hand. The mapping from a source label to the video label it implies is in the script's docstring.

**99** batch-2 videos. **3** are `out_of_scope` and excluded from every number (section 14), leaving **96**; **89** of those have a source label that implies a video label, **74** of them outside M4Health.

- Agreement with the implied label, M4Health excluded: **23 / 74 (31%)**, Cohen's kappa **0.13**
- M4Health on its own: **1 / 15 (7%)**. Not pooled: M4Health labels every video in its list FALSE, memes and adverts included, so its label is not a verdict on claims.
- For reference, all sources pooled: 24 / 89 (27%), kappa 0.10

MISINFO counts both bases; a video whose only false claim is an untested efficacy claim is MISINFO with `misinfo_basis` `unproven` (section 9) and is always reviewed.

The sources judged whole videos, by a fact-checker's reading or a dataset's annotation scheme; the derived label comes from per-claim evidence under section 7, where an untested claim is INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE rather than false. So disagreement here is mostly a measure of how often a source's 'false' rests on claims the literature has not tested, not of labelling error.

**Not a blind comparison.** The batch-2 labellers had seen every source label before writing claims (PLAN.md, batch 2). If that biased anything it would push towards agreement, so the true agreement is unlikely to be higher than this. The batch-2 blind subset in REVIEW.md is where an independent number has to come from.
