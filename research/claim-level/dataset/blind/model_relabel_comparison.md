# Blind relabel comparison (blind_model_pass_v1 vs first pass)

> **Model-model agreement between two Claude labelling passes. Not human inter-annotator agreement; errors may be correlated.**

41 blind-sample videos (REVIEW.md). First pass: `claims.csv` / `videos.csv` (`labelling_pipeline_v1`). Second pass: `blind/model_relabel_*.csv` (`blind_model_pass_v1`), labelled from transcripts and live PubMed only, committed before the main CSVs were opened (see `blind/READ_LOG.md`). Generated by `scripts/compare_blind.py`.

## Summary

- To adjudicate: **7** video-level disagreements, **27** matched-claim disagreements (stance or verdict), **46** claims extracted by only one pass (17 first, 29 model).

## 1. Claim extraction

| | first pass | model pass |
|---|---|---|
| claims | 141 | 153 |
| matched | 124 | 124 |
| unmatched | 17 | 29 |

Matched share: 88% of first-pass claims, 81% of model-pass claims.

**Matching rule.** Within a video, one-to-one. Similarity is the overlap coefficient of content words (lower-cased, accents stripped, 3+ letters, stop words removed). A pair is a candidate when the two time windows overlap (each widened by 3 s) and similarity ≥ 0.34, or similarity ≥ 0.6 at any time; candidates are taken greedily, best first. 8 pairs were then matched by hand on meaning (listed in `MANUAL_MATCH` with reasons), e.g. the same claim phrased as a negation in the other pass. A compound claim in one pass matches at most one of the split claims in the other; the rest show up as unmatched.

## 4. Every disagreement

Notes say which side looks right and why, as an input to adjudication. Nothing here changes `claims.csv` or `videos.csv`; the project lead decides.

**Systematic causes seen.** (1) Model-pass misses on v114 and v122: its transcript viewer truncated long caption lines and printed only the first caption lines, so later on-screen claims were never seen. (2) Granularity: one pass splits a sentence that the other keeps whole, and most unmatched claims are this. (3) The UNSUPPORTED/INSUFFICIENT boundary when evidence exists for an ingredient or a related intervention (v113, v149) and when a claim is detox (v120); the model applied it inconsistently. (4) Both stance disagreements are polarity artefacts (one pass stores the negation of the other's claim), so stance agrees fully in substance.
