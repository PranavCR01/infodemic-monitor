Why these label colours

MISINFO Rust-orange, warning triangle. Warm and serious without alarm-red. Shares its hue with CONTRADICTED because both mean “false claim is promoted / evidence says false”.

DEBUNKED Teal, shield-check. Opposite side of the wheel from MISINFO (orange↔teal survives deuteranopia and protanopia better than red↔green). Different icon shape and a “corrects misinformation” line on the deciding claim.

NO-MISINFO Institutional blue, check-circle. Shares hue with SUPPORTED.

CANNOT RECOGNIZE Warm grey, dashed border, dashed question icon. Dashed = “deliberately not decided”, used again for INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE and UNCLEAR stance.

INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE No fill, dashed grey. Visually the emptiest chip: it means nothing tests the claim.

REFUTES Stance chips are colourless. Stance describes the speaker; colour is reserved for judgements, so REFUTES never reads as “bad”.

Known limit: MISINFO and DEBUNKED tints have nearly equal lightness (relative luminance 0.81 vs 0.81), so in greyscale print they are told apart by icon and text only. That meets WCAG 1.4.1, and the export always prints the label name.

Fonts: keep the pair, add a mono

Source Serif 4 for page titles and the one-sentence explanation only. It gives the result the voice of a written finding, which suits a WHO audience. Not used in dense UI.

IBM Plex Sans for all UI. Open counters and distinct I/l/1 at 12–14 px, good Latin Extended and Cyrillic coverage, Plex Sans Arabic and Devanagari exist if the tool is localised.

IBM Plex Mono (new) for timestamps, file names, IDs. Tabular figures keep timestamp columns aligned.
